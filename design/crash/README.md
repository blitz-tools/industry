# Crash mockup set

The reaction-terminal set — same pages, layout, and components as the
reference standard ([`../typhoon/`](../typhoon/README.md), see
[`../README.md`](../README.md) for the binding convention), populated
with the Strong Crash Booster ×100 chain. It exercises what no
previous set could: a deliverable that **rides a three-tier
biochemical reaction stack** (reactions hold ~95% of the plan's
slot-hours — the inverse of every build-dominated profile), the
**cross-drug DAG** (the improved tier consumes two sibling drug
lines, the strong tier a third — the first non-tree chain, with the
Frentix line scheduling a tier later than its standard-tier
siblings), the **batch cascade** (15/15/15/12/12 yields aligning
near-exactly at ×100, surplus only at the final run), the
**second absent-copy case with a different cause** (no copy activity
exists — originals throughout), and a from-zero acquisition story
that is five market rows (three gases + two reagents) plus ~21 cheap
originals.

| File | Shows |
|---|---|
| `planner.html` | Commodity-first search and target, Plan participants (the fourth-reactor inclusion; Drug Manufacturing flagged as held by nobody), 6-type blueprint tier (formulas ×4 per line — originals are the parallelism), intermediates summary with the stock-collapsed fuel blocks, raw summaries (5 gap rows), reaction-stack summary card (the batch cascade table — this set's analog of the decryptor/grade tables) |
| `build-plan.html` | Reaction-dominated wave bar (three 2.4 d tiers + a 17% manufacturing sliver + the acquisition front, 9.1 d), DAG-shaped chain flow (Frentix joins at phase 3), phase tree with cross-drug gating notes, the absent copy plan (no copy activity exists), required blueprints (~21 originals + the booster BP acquisition pin), gaps-only gas acquisition, wave-1 allocation across four reactors |
| `build-plan-active.html` | The same plan mid-flight (day 6.5 of 9.1, 71%): standard + improved tiers complete, strong + Frentix running level at 52%, the **intermediates ledger** (delivered vs consumed per drug line — this set's active-page contribution), zero residue until the cascade's final run, the manufacturing cap queued on any Industry line |

**2026-11-22 fan-out**: every page carries the standard 2026-11-22
conventions — the planner's Plan facilities and sell-side market
sections, the build plan's Market panel (build-vs-buy verdicts +
estimated profit) and wave pager, the active page's running market
readout with open verdicts — populated with this chain's data. All Ƶ figures are illustrative
until M15's price sync; full section notes live on the Typhoon
reference set.

## Data provenance

- Quantities, phases, runs, and the batch cascade come from
  `reference/chains/CRASH-CHAIN.md` (the model runs at ×100 against
  the live SDE mirror). Formulas, consumptions, yields (15/15/15/12/12),
  durations (3 h reactions, 33.3 h max job), and the cross-drug
  structure are SDE data; the booster BP's acquisition path and
  biochemical facility requirements are recorded pins.
- Blueprint ownership from the live `blueprints` table, pulled
  2026-11-20: nothing in the booster world is owned — six types
  missing (~21 originals with the one-per-reactor parallelism). Gas,
  Spirits, and Hydrochloric Acid at zero are real; so is the
  stock that collapses the fuel-block subtree (2,398 Nitrogen /
  1,027 Helium blocks against 1,840 / 460 needed) and covers Water
  (110,337) and Megacyte (5,117).
- **Skills simulated only for Drug Manufacturing 5** — nobody on the
  roster holds it (the sole live gap). Every reaction gate
  (Reactions 2/3/4) is held live at 5 by four characters, mirroring
  the prior sets' arrangement in reverse: the reaction gates live,
  the manufacturing gate simulated.
- Wave decomposition (the three equal reaction tiers, the Frentix
  line inside W3, the 9.1 d chain end), intermediates movements,
  and market-average figures are **invented for the wireframe** —
  anchored to the chain document's durations but illustrative in
  between; the real waves are the M9 scheduler's output.
