Batch 1 landed below its expectation — 8 successes from 30 attempts against E 10.2, a 26%-probability outcome — which is precisely the branch the re-arm exists for. The binomial is memoryless: batch 2's 8 attempts carry the same 34% each, sized so P(≥ 2) reaches 82%. If batch 2 also falls short, the plan blocks at the budget ceiling with the owner decision — extend, escalate decryptor, buy BPCs, or descope to 8 rigs.

wireframe The loop-state card is the retry-semantics surface: progress meters for successes and attempt budget, the re-arm with its cause, and both residue directions while short. The decision point fires only at budget exhaustion — mid-loop there is nothing to decide, the default policy is simply running. A blocked card would replace the re-arm line with the four owner options (extend · escalate · buy BPCs · descope); spend never auto-escalates. Successes are read from completed industry jobs in the synced jobs table — actuals against expectation, never a deterministic promise.
